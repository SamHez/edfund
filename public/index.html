We are EdFund Uganda Foundation

Who We Are

EdFund Uganda Foundation, Inc. is a US-based 501(c)(3) private educational nonprofit dedicated to restoring historic education infrastructure and bringing new technology into the classrooms of Eastern Uganda.

Many schools built in the 1950s fell into disrepair or closed following the political instability of the Idi Amin era. With inadequate government funding to renovate these structures, EdFund’s school rebuilding work fills a critical infrastructure gap.

By supporting institutions such as Kamuli Township Primary School, which serves 2,500 children, EdFund connects improvements to school buildings with better access to education and technology.

Our Mission

To advance the social, personal and economic development of children and youth in Uganda through greater access to education, technology and other life-changing resources.

The place behind the purpose

Why Uganda?

From the streets of Kamuli to the classrooms where its children learn, see why this work matters.

Chapter 01 / 04

Our Journey of Transformation

These photographs show Kamuli Municipality as recently as 2020: unpaved roads, animals sharing the main roadways and vendors selling goods along the edges of the main street.

With more than 50,000 residents, Kamuli Municipality is the administrative centre of a district with a population of roughly half a million.

Chapter 02 / 04

Progress on the Infrastructure though Challenges remain.

Government investment has improved infrastructure in recent years, and many streets in Kamuli Municipality are now paved. Yet poverty remains a significant challenge.

World Bank estimates based on the 2019/20 household survey put around 42% of Uganda’s population below the then international poverty line of US$2.15 per person per day (2017 purchasing power parity).

Historical poverty estimate · World Bank ↗

Chapter 03 / 04

School buildings also need investment.

Public funding for school improvements remains limited.

Older school facilities need substantial investment, and many buildings date from the initial school construction of the 1950s.

Chapter 04 / 04

Rebuilding spaces. Opening opportunities.

EdFund Uganda Foundation stepped in to help fill that gap.

Over the past 15 years, EdFund-supported work has helped rebuild classrooms and dormitories at Kamuli Township Primary School, serving 2,500 boys and girls in grades P1–P7.

Before & after · The kitchen

A better place to cook and eat.

From open-fire cooking and outdoor meals to a dedicated kitchen and dining hall.

The 2010 photographs show meals being prepared in large drums over open fires. Cooking took place among smoke and firewood, with the pots resting directly above the flames.

By 2015, the school kitchen had fuel-efficient stoves built into raised cooking surfaces. The photographs show the new cooking area and a cook using one of the fitted stoves: a clear change in the spaces used to prepare school meals.

Before & after · The dining space

A dedicated place to share a meal.

The earlier photographs document both meal preparation over open fires and children eating outdoors beside the old kitchen. Together, they show the conditions surrounding a daily part of school life.

The completed kitchen and dining hall appears in the 2012 photographs. Inside, children sit together at tables and benches in a covered dining space. Outside, the new building brings the cooking and dining facilities together on the school campus.

These before-and-after views show how rebuilding extends beyond classrooms to the everyday spaces where children eat and spend time together.

Before & after · Girls’ and boys’ dormitories

Better places to rest and grow.

Rebuilding school accommodation is part of EdFund’s wider response at Kamuli Township Primary School. Alongside classrooms and shared facilities, dormitories are an important part of everyday school life for girls and boys.

The before photographs show the girls’ older accommodation: beds arranged in existing rooms beneath the building’s roof structure. They provide a closer view of the living spaces included in the school’s rebuilding story.

The after photographs focus on the new girls’ dormitory, showing its interior with rows of bunk beds and the 2025 opening ceremony outside the building. Together, these views document the change from the older accommodation to the new dormitory.

Before & after · Classroom reconstruction

Better classrooms. Brighter beginnings.

Classroom rebuilding is central to EdFund’s work at Kamuli Township Primary School. Renewing existing buildings connects the foundation’s education mission with the spaces children use to learn each day.

The before photograph shows an older classroom block with weathered walls and timber supports along its covered walkway. It records the condition of the building before renovation.

The after photograph brings together two views of the renovated classroom building. Renewed walls, windows and a covered walkway show the transformation from the older structure to a refreshed learning environment.
